A workflow re-analyzed the full current gamut: 65 three-bot runs (crafting / magic / smith-team × 22 models, 45-minute horizon), one agent characterizing each run's cooperation from the timed team-chat transcript + per-bot skill split. ✅ The two SDK fixes from the prior round both landed

The prior analysis flagged ~80-char chat truncation (#1) and ground-item despawn (#2) as the top cooperation taxes. Both were addressed in the engine image (NODE_MAX_MESSAGE_LENGTH=500, NODE_OBJ_DESPAWN_SCALE=20000 ≈ items linger ~200×). The new gamut confirms they took effect:

maxMessageLength → 500: working

Across 7,117 chat messages: median 75, p95 171, p99 257, max 405 chars. Zero messages exceed 500 and there is no pile-up at the cap — nothing is being truncated. Multi-sentence coordination (roles, coords, ore ratios) now flows intact; the old 80-char cap would have clipped the trailing coords/counts that handoffs depend on. No in-band "your message got cut off" complaints remain.

objDespawnScale → 20000: working

Only ~1 message across all 65 runs asserts a confirmed ground-item loss, vs 32 anticipatory "grab it before it despawns" pings and 245 successful pickup/collection reports. The top crafting runs (Fable 5 50,582 XP, Sonnet 5 30,194) are only physically possible if teammate-dropped materials persisted for the full run. Caveat: models still behave as if items vanish fast — they rush handoffs and pre-emptively re-collect out of RuneScape prior — so the fix removed the real loss but not the paranoia-driven drop-churn.

Headline: strategy is saturated — execution is now the whole game

Essentially all 22 models independently discover the reward-optimal "hero + 2 feeders" funnel (one bot does the scoring skill; two gather and drop materials) within ~1–2 minutes of chat, in every event. So score variance is a near-pure measurement of long-horizon embodied execution — pathfinding, drop-point discipline, pickup verification, resource stockpiling, role stability over 45 minutes — not planning. A handful even verbalize the scoring rule mid-run (Sonnet 5: "only my crafting xp counts for score"; Opus 4.7: "alphabetical last = stays put").

Strategy taxonomy

  • Clean hero-funnel (designated hero + 2 committed feeders): one bot camps a fixed station while two gather/ferry and end at L1; the intended, reward-optimal pattern — the sole strategy of every strong run (opus47, gpt55, sonnet5, fable5, gemini31, gemini35flash).
  • Fast-lock convergence: multiple bots independently propose the identical funnel within 20-90s and accept on their next line with no back-and-forth (fable5 25s, opus47 <100s, gemini31 20s) — the strongest single predictor of a top score.
  • Dynamic feeder load-balancing: the two gatherers split by resource/zone and re-balance as the hero's bottleneck shifts (fable5 'you take coal, I take mithril'; sonnet5 hauler specialization) — a rare second-order refinement above the base funnel.
  • Scoring-rule verbalization: a bot explicitly states the reward function to prevent teammates wasting XP on their own accounts (sonnet5 'only my crafting xp counts'; gpt55 '1 crafter only for score'; opus47 'alphabetical last = stays put') — evidence of genuine strategic reasoning over pattern-matching.
  • Pooled-resource / autocast shortcut: buying a shared staff-of-air or pooling gold so the hero needn't carry reagents (opus adopted a staff-of-air; fable5/glm52 proposed but abandoned it) — high-leverage when executed, usually abandoned for lack of funds.
  • Emergent hero-reassignment: a support bot correctly recognizes it has become the strongest hero and the team re-designates mid-run (kimi at min-33; opus48's agentc as backup smith) — a legitimate adaptive strategy, but usually surfaced too late to fully capitalize.
  • Ad-hoc anti-despawn handoff protocol: 'stand on the tile and wait for visual confirmation before dropping' invented to counter ground-drop fragility (gpt55, gpt54, gemini31, codex53) — a coordination tax the better teams pay deliberately.
  • Redundant-parallel feeders: both supporters run the identical farm→buy/mine→drop loop instead of specializing — a weaker but still-functional variant (kimi, sonnet46, codex53 magic).
  • Deliberate-abandonment to parallel solo grinds: the funnel is explicitly dropped and all three grind independently (opus crafting 'craft your own', opus/sonnet46/opus45 smith) — a strategy choice that is anti-optimal and is really a cooperation failure wearing a plan's clothing.

Failure taxonomy

  • Talked-but-never-executed plan: correct funnel agreed in chat, no bot ever takes a scoring action; all finish at floor (qwen35, qwen3max, gpt54nano crafting, haiku) — the benchmark's cautionary tale. Frequency: ~6-8 runs, concentrated in the weak tier.
  • Silent/absent hero or third bot: the designated hero goes dark or a bot never speaks, collapsing a 3-bot team to ~1.5 (opus48 silent smith, gpt54nano silent hero, sonnet46 silent agentc, qwen3max ≥1 mute bot every family, sonnet5's agentb after 23:15). Frequency: very high — appears in most weak/mixed runs.
  • Fragmentation / funnel dilution: a feeder starts smithing/crafting/casting its own materials, splitting scored output across 2-3 accounts (opus, opus45, sonnet46, glm52, gpt54mini, kimi). Frequency: the dominant smith/crafting failure; ~7-8 runs. Damaging in smith/craft, harmless-to-diluting in magic.
  • Role thrashing / amnesia: 'who is the hero?' re-litigated 4-10x and/or re-asked 15-40min after settling (glm52 caster flipped 5-6x, gpt54 ~10x, sonnet45 4x, geminiflash/kimi late re-asks). Frequency: ~8-10 runs; the primary mixed-tier discipline leak.
  • Drop-location churn: the rendezvous tile relocated 3-12x due to pathing blocks, forcing the hero to chase scattered piles (codex53 magic ~12x, gpt54 7x, opus47, gpt54nano, opus48 across 3 sites). Frequency: near-universal, even in strong runs.
  • Talked-but-never-verified handoffs: large material piles announced with no pickup-confirmation loop; much despawns unused (glm ~9 wool drops/1 spin, haiku 35+ hides/0 crafts, codex53 triple-digit hide piles, glm52 '42 items untouched'). Frequency: very high.
  • Ratio/rune-type imbalance: feeders deliver the wrong mix — air-vs-mind oscillation in magic, iron-vs-coal in smith — starving the hero of one input while it holds surplus of the other (sonnet5, opus47, gpt55, fable5, gemini31, codex53). Frequency: ~10+ runs; the main throughput leak among strong teams.
  • Deaths wiping stockpiles: king-scorpion (smith/mine) or dark-wizard/wolf (magic) deaths cost pickaxes/runes and multi-minute recovery walks (fable5, opus47, gpt54, opus48, sonnet5, gemini31). Frequency: near-universal in smith and common in magic.
  • Terminal stuck-loop / spam: a bot hard-locks repeating one identical line for the productive tail (glm 130x 'out of mind runes', gpt54 138x handoff, opus48 ~900 idle spams with frozen XP, geminiflash ~90x, sonnet45 ~24x). Frequency: ~6-8 runs; burns the endgame.
  • Navigation / world-knowledge failure: bots can't find the sheep pen, ore rock, mine entrance, or rune shop at all (qwen35 all three families, gpt54nano ladders, haiku sealed castle). Frequency: ~5 runs, the weak tier's root cause.
  • Economic-bottleneck-discovered-late: coins for tolls/tanning, needle+thread, or gold-for-runes not stockpiled proactively, stalling the hero mid-run (opus47 ~26min, glm52 ~28min, gemini35flash 12-min needle stall, kimi 25-min rune starvation). Frequency: high across families.
  • Post-goal wasted effort: scoring item locked before halfway, then 40%+ of the run spent chasing a mathematically unreachable higher tier (opus47, gpt55, gpt54, sonnet5, gemini35flash, gemini31 — all smith). Frequency: near-universal among STRONG runs; a distinctive strong-tier inefficiency rather than a collapse.

Key insights

  • Strategy is fully saturated; the benchmark measures execution, not planning. ~20-22 of 22 models found the reward-optimal funnel in <2 minutes in every family — so any score variance is a pure measurement of long-horizon embodied coordination discipline, making these unusually clean 'plan-held-constant' natural experiments.
  • Cooperation ability does NOT generalize for the middle tier — it is task-contingent. opus is best-in-class in magic (28) yet fragments in craft and smith; glm52 posts a 27836 crafting outlier but total collapses (2, 16) elsewhere; sonnet46 and codex53 each swing 2+ tiers. The bottleneck is whether disciplined execution survives a specific game's logistics friction, not a portable 'teamwork' skill.
  • The Opus lineage is the sharpest intra-provider inconsistency in the study: opus47 executes cleanly everywhere, but plain opus, opus45, and opus48 repeatedly abandon or dilute the funnel despite naming it correctly. This suggests the frontier gain is specifically in *sustaining* multi-agent execution over 44 minutes, not in strategic insight — which all versions already have.
  • 'Single-bot-carried' is genuinely ambiguous and must be disambiguated by concentrationIntended + who scored. opus45 smith (2000, by accident, feeders redundantly reached the same item) reads identically to opus47 smith (2000, by design) on the scoreboard — a scoring degeneracy where a broken run and a clean run tie. Reward alone cannot certify cooperation.
  • Magic is the cleanest cooperation test because it has NO lone-wolf-scored-high escape hatch: a single unfed caster cannot buy runes fast enough alone, so every high score genuinely required two working feeders. Smith and crafting are noisier — a defecting feeder can accidentally become the winning account (opus, opus45, sonnet46), rewarding fragmentation.
  • Strong models systematically waste the back half of the run. In smith, nearly every top team (opus47, gpt55, gpt54, sonnet5, gemini31, gemini35flash) locked its scoring item before halfway then spent 40%+ chasing an unreachable tier — a horizon-planning / stopping-criterion failure distinct from coordination, and a clear target for improvement that would not change scores but would reveal better meta-reasoning.
  • qwen3max exhibits a qualitatively different failure than every other model: it is not messy coordination but the ABSENCE of coordination — ≥1 of 3 bots sends zero chat messages in all three families, and any concentration is incidental to one bot working in silent isolation. It should be treated as a non-participant baseline, not a weak cooperator.
  • The ground-drop handoff mechanic is the dominant execution tax and is where good teams differentiate: the strong tier independently invents anti-despawn 'stand-on-tile-and-confirm' protocols (gpt55, gemini31, codex53), while the weak tier drops-and-hopes with no pickup-confirmation loop (glm, haiku, glm52) — often the single deciding factor between a high score and a floor score given identical plans.
  • Terminal spam loops (a bot repeating one identical line 59-900x) are a recurring, model-agnostic degradation that corrupts chat as a coordination signal exactly when it matters, and appears even in otherwise-decent runs (opus48's crafter froze XP at 1380 vs actual 13800 while spamming ~900 idle messages; gpt54's 138x handoff loop was half its transcript). This looks like an agent-loop / retry pathology worth fixing at the harness level.
  • Reward magnitude structure hides coordination quality differently per family: crafting reward is dominated by one crafter's throughput so even cq3 runs post large numbers (fable5 50582 at cq3), whereas magic caps the entire family at 28 because gold generation is universally slow — meaning cross-family score comparison must be ordinal-within-family, and coordQuality is a more honest cross-family cooperation signal than raw reward.

Per-event synthesis

Crafting — full synthesis, coordination-vs-score, failure modes, standouts

Overview

The crafting-team benchmark rewards funneling all crafting XP onto a single account (topShare=1 is optimal), which means the correct macro-strategy is a "hero crafter + 2 gatherer/couriers" funnel: one bot camps a crafting station while two teammates gather raw materials (wool, cowhides, coins for tanning/tolls) and ferry them via in-game ground drops. Strikingly, nearly every one of the 21 models identified this strategy — often within the first 60-120 seconds of chat. The differentiator across the family was almost never *planning* and almost always *execution*: whether the drop-and-pickup supply chain actually converted gathered material into banked XP.

Dominant archetype: the hero-funnel

The single dominant archetype is "designated crafter + 2 gatherer/couriers," appearing in essentially all runs. concentrationIntended=true in 19 of 21 runs (only gpt54mini and sonnet45's sibling qwen35 recorded it as unintended/absent). This near-universal convergence on the reward-aligned plan is itself the headline finding: the models understand the incentive. sonnet5 and gpt55 even had a bot explicitly articulate the scoring rule mid-game ("only my crafting xp counts for score," "1 crafter only for score"), the clearest evidence of genuine strategic reasoning rather than pattern-matching a RuneScape tutorial.

A minor sub-pattern was the material pipeline chosen: wool-spinning (fast to start, low XP/action) versus the cowhide -> tan -> leather chain (higher XP but needs coins for tolls/tanning, tools like needle+thread/chisel, and a longer courier loop). The highest scorers all ran the leather pipeline (fable5 50582, opus47 29534, sonnet5 30194, glm52 27836, gpt55 23985, codex53 19872). Teams that stayed on wool tended to score low (glm 550, gemini35flash 4061), partly because wool XP is thin and partly because the leather chain forced tighter division of labor.

Coordination quality vs. score

Coordination quality clearly tracked score, but imperfectly, and the reward's own structure explains the noise. Because the score is dominated by the single crafter's throughput, a run can post a very high number with only mediocre (3/5) coordination as long as the hero never fully starves. The top scorers — fable5 (50582, cq3), opus47 (29534, cq3), glm52 (27836, cq3), gpt55 (23985, cq3), codex53 (19872, cq3) — nearly all sit at coordination quality 3, not 4. The task and its single dominant archetype

The magic-team benchmark scores a three-bot team by the highest Magic level achieved on any single account, so the reward function structurally rewards funneling all output to one "hero" caster while the other two bots act as gatherers/suppliers. Remarkably, every single one of the 22 models independently discovered this "hero + 2 feeders" archetype — there is essentially no strategic diversity at the top level. All 22 have `concentrationIntended: true`. The variance in this family is therefore almost entirely about *execution quality of a shared plan*, not about strategy selection. This makes magic-team an unusually clean natural experiment: the plan is a constant, so reward becomes a pure measure of coordination discipline and logistics throughput.

The shared plan is always some version of: one bot self-nominates (or is nominated) as the caster and camps near a rune shop (Aubury's in Varrock, Betty's in Port Sarim, or Lumbridge spawn), while the other two run a coin-farm (pickpocketing men, killing goblins/dark wizards) → buy-runes → drop-at-rendezvous loop, feeding air+mind runes for Wind Strike. The caster spam-casts to grind XP. Nobody invented a materially different economy (no meaningful runecrafting/altar pipelines, no autocast staff that actually paid off, no bank-based logistics).

Coordination quality strongly tracks score

The correlation between `coordinationQuality` and `reward` is strong and monotone at the extremes. Every model that scored below 5 (qwen35=1, qwen3max=1, glm52=2, glm=3, geminiflash=3, haiku=4) had coordinationQuality of 1 or 2. Every model that scored 15+ (gpt55=15, sonnet46=16, fable5=18, opus47=19, gemini35flash=20, sonnet5=21, opus=28) had coordinationQuality of 3 or 4 (mostly 4). The single best predictor of a good score is whether the team locked the caster role early and never re-litigated it, combined with whether material sharing was rated "effective" rather than "attempted-ineffective."

The clearest illustration is the two total failures. qwen35 (reward 1) and qwen3max (reward 1) both have `planStatus: agreed-not-followed` and `workConcentration: fragmented` — the *only* two models in the family with those values. This is the pure "chat-level agreement without in-game follow-through" failure — the benchmark's cautionary tale that talking about a plan is not the same as any bot taking a scoring action.

The role-stability axis is the real differentiator

Because the archetype is fixed, the sharpest fault line among the mid-and-high performers is role stability. The models with `planStatus: agreed-and-followed` (gemini31, gemini35flash, kimi, opus, opus45, sonnet5, sonnet46) cluster at the high end of both coordinationQuality (all 4) and reward (9, 20, 10, 28, 12, 21, 16). Even strong models leaked coordination discipline over time. gpt54 (reward 12) settled the role by 1:15 but then re-asked "who is main caster?" roughly ten more times, and half of its transcript (138 of 276 messages) was agentc stuck in a repetitive "come for handoff" spam loop. opus47 (reward 19) is the interesting near-exception: it kept `agreed-partially-followed` and coordinationQuality 3 (not 4) because support bots wandered far off-rendezvous and two deaths cost inventories — yet it still scored 19 because when material *did* land, it landed in bulk. A striking and near-universal finding: casting speed was never the limit — gold-to-rune throughput was. Overview

The smith-team task drops three concurrent bots (agenta/agentb/agentc) of a single model into RuneScape with a scoring function that rewards the single highest-value smithed item on any one account. The dominant-strategy insight the task probes is subtle: because only the best item on one account counts, the score-optimal play is to concentrate all mined ore onto one "hero" smith and level that account as high as possible, while the other two act as pure ore-gatherers/haulers who ferry material via in-game ground drops (there is no direct trade; sharing is drop-and-pickup). Nearly every model *discovered this strategy in chat* — the "designated hero-smith + 2 gatherers" archetype was proposed by 20 of 22 runs — but the models separated sharply on whether they could *execute* it. The gap between "we agreed on the plan" and "the plan is what produced the score" is the single most discriminating axis in this family.

Dominant archetypes

Three archetypes recur. (1) Clean hero + 2 gatherers — one bot smiths exclusively at a fixed furnace/anvil while the other two mine and drop ore at an agreed tile, never smithing themselves. This is the intended concentration pattern and it correlates with every high score: opus47, gpt55, fable5, sonnet5, gemini31, gemini35flash, codex53, gpt54. (2) Fragmented parallel smithing — the plan named one hero, but one or both "feeders" started smithing their own ore for XP, splitting output across two or three accounts. This is the most common failure and it capped scores at whatever a single accidental account happened to reach: opus, opus45, opus48, sonnet46, gpt54mini. (3) Collapse / never-executed — role assignment thrashed, drop-and-pickup never closed, or bots couldn't even find ore rocks, leaving the score as a near-zero fallback item (usually a bronze_axe, cost 16, often credited at elapsed 00:00 i.e. incidental to the run): glm, glm52, haiku, qwen35, qwen3max, gpt54nano.

Coordination quality tracked score strongly but not perfectly. The two coordQuality-5 runs (opus47, gemini31) both scored 2000, and the coordQuality-4 cluster (gpt55 2600, fable5 2600, sonnet5 750, gemini35flash 2000, codex53 2000, gpt54 3-but-really-4-behavior 2000) all landed genuine mid-to-high scores. Every coordQuality 0–1 run (qwen3max 16, haiku 16, qwen35 16, gpt54nano 28) bottomed out near the floor. The task's key ambiguity — "single-bot-carried" can be brilliant or broken — resolves cleanly against concentrationIntended plus the summaries. Deliberate, well-executed concentration: opus47, gpt55, fable5, sonnet5, gemini31, gemini35flash, codex53, gpt54 all have concentrationIntended=true AND the hero was fed by committed gatherers who ended at smithing L1. What the 18 runs reveal

Under the new from-scratch bootstrap conditions (every bot starts identical: bronze pickaxe+axe, level-30 melee, and *nothing* else — no coins, runes, ore, or tools), cooperation stopped being a nice-to-have and became the load-bearing mechanic. A single bot cannot both bootstrap a resource economy (mine/thieve/kill for coins → buy tools/runes → process) *and* specialize hard enough to score. So the interesting signal is which models actually stood up a gather/earn → buy → funnel → specialist chain and sustained it for the hour, versus which flailed on role election, pathfinding, or the lossy ground-drop transfer channel.

The verdict is sharp and model-consistent across events. Gemini 3.5 Flash, GPT-5.5, and Claude Sonnet 5 reliably built genuine supply chains in all three events and posted the top scores (Gemini smith mithril_med_helm 780gp with the smith at Mining 35; Sonnet crafting lvl21/34,732xp; GPT-5.5 smith steel_platebody 2000gp / crafting lvl9). Opus 4.8 built real chains but bled a large fraction of supplier throughput to method-thrash and despawn (crafting: 14 min lost to wool→gold→leather thrash + toll bug, still reached lvl17/25,336xp). GLM-5.2 was the clear laggard: leadership thrashed in every event (three simultaneous self-appointed smiths; a never-resolved b-vs-c crafter fight; a magic run where the caster never secured air runes and finished at Magic level 1). GPT-5.4-mini was middling-to-poor — it worked in smith/crafting-partial but collapsed to three-solo-grinders in crafting (topShare 0.475, all three lvl1) and late role-thrash in magic.

The throughline: the differentiator was not in-game mechanical skill (everyone hit the same pathfinding walls, toll gates, and despawns) but social/organizational discipline — locking one specialist fast and keeping two teammates in a disciplined earn-buy-haul loop for a full hour. Teams that locked roles in <2 minutes and never re-litigated leadership (Gemini, GPT-5.5, Sonnet) converted; teams that let two bots both claim the scoring role (GLM, GPT-5.4-mini) burned the hour refereeing instead of building an economy.

Chat / SDK-layer issues — for the SDK maintainer

Chat / SDK-layer problems for the maintainer

RESOLVED 2026-07-01 1. Hard message-length truncation (~78-80 chars) — universal and load-bearing. Every single one of the 18 runs reports it, and multiple confirm it is a *transport* cap, not a display artifact: Sonnet smith — "reward.json chat[].text fields are identically capped, confirming a transport-level cap not a display artifact"; GPT-5.4-mini smith — "reward.json chat entries confirm the truncation is in the stored text field." Concrete damage: coordinates and quantities — the exact payloads coordination depends on — get clipped off the end. GLM smith: "steel platebody (2000g\*\*\*\*\*ill" and "Mine ore, smelt bars at f" (furnace coord lost). Agents *noticed and complained in-band*: Gemini smith [01:05] "Agentb, your message got cut off. Who should be the smith?"; Sonnet magic [35:50] "please always include coords when dropping" — literally asking teammates to re-send coords truncation had eaten. This is the #1 fix: raise or eliminate the cap, or at minimum guarantee trailing coordinate tokens survive.

2. Ground-drop is the ONLY item-transfer channel, and it is lossy. There is no direct trade/give. Every rune, ore, hide, and coin handoff goes through drop→coord-broadcast→pickup, which fails via (a) despawn before pickup, (b) empty "0-item" transfers (GPT-5.5 crafting "recovered 0 wool"/"moved 13/0 wool"; Gemini crafting "Dropped 0 soft leathers" for a 13-min stretch), and (c) server-side visibility lag where dropper and picker see different ground state (GPT-5.5 smith [28:40] "items still visible to me on shared tile; waiting for server visibility delay"). In GLM crafting this single-channel design *structurally defeated the whole task* — suppliers stranded across the toll gate had no way to deliver. A direct player-to-player trade primitive would eliminate the largest source of wasted work across all three events.

3. gameMessages polling staleness. Cross-bot state is only observable via getState().gameMessages.slice(-N) on a polling loop, so bots act on stale state and re-ask constantly. GLM smith [09:50] "i don't see anyone smithing at the furnace" acting on stale state; Opus smith re-asked "whats ur smithing lvl?" many times (lines 79/84/108/114) "because they could not reliably see teammate state." Bottom line

Bottom line

  • Organizational discipline, not game skill, decided the round. Every team hit the same despawns, toll gates, coal/rune starvation, and pathfinding walls. The winners (Sonnet 5, Gemini 3.5 Flash, GPT-5.5) all locked one specialist in under 2 minutes and never re-litigated leadership; the losers (GLM-5.2, GPT-5.4-mini) let two bots both claim the scoring role and burned 10-40 minutes refereeing instead of bootstrapping an economy.
  • Cooperation was genuinely load-bearing this round, and the top models delivered it. The cleanest proofs: Gemini's smith hit Smithing 57 at Mining 35, and Opus's smith hit Smithing 49 at Mining 1 — neither could have self-supplied. Sonnet's crafter reached lvl21/34,732xp from a lvl-1 start only because two teammates bootstrapped the entire toolchain and hide supply. topShare=1.0 with two lvl-1 suppliers is the *correct* success shape here, not idleness.
  • The most egregious failures were resource-bootstrap and role-collapse, not mechanics. GLM-5.2 magic's caster finished Magic level 1 with zero air runes — the team never secured the one resource the task required (suppliers dropped the wrong runes; one teammate was trapped in a tower the whole hour). GLM crafting collapsed to a single self-sufficient carry (427xp, both teammates 0xp, funnel "added nothing"). GPT-5.4-mini crafting collapsed to three solo grinders (all lvl1, topShare 0.475).
  • The ground-drop transfer channel is the SDK's biggest tax on cooperation. With no direct trade primitive, despawn, empty "0-item" pickups, and server visibility lag destroyed a large share of supplier output in nearly every run — and in GLM crafting it *structurally defeated the task* (suppliers stranded across the toll gate). A player-to-player trade API is the single highest-leverage fix.
  • The 80-char chat truncation actively degraded coordination — it clipped the trailing coordinates/counts that handoffs depend on, and agents complained about it in-band ("your message got cut off. Who should be the smith?"; "please always include coords when dropping"). Combined with gameMessages polling staleness and asterisk-masking of numbers/words, the comms layer forced redundant re-asks that ate turns. Raising the cap and adding a teammate-state query would recover real signal.
